After the 2025 record, sales of **live cattle** (Combined Nomenclature code 0102) from Portugal to Spain fell back in 2026. Adding up the months from **January to August** (the latest month published by Eurostat):

| January–August | Value | Weight | Average value per kg |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | €44.6 M | 13,881 t | €3.22 |
| 2025 | €58.9 M | 12,335 t | €4.77 |
| **2026** | **€38.8 M** | **7,838 t** | **€4.96** |

Compared with 2025, **4,497 fewer tonnes** left (−36%) and the value fell by **€20.0 million** (−34%). The weight exported in these eight months is the lowest since 2023 (3,938 t), before the sharp rise of 2024.

The average value per kilo — the declared value divided by the declared weight — **did not follow the fall**: it stood at €4.96/kg, slightly above 2025 and about 54% above 2024. In other words, in 2026 the decline came from volume, not from unit value.

**What this shows — and what it does not show.** It shows fewer live animals (by weight) leaving for Spain, at a value per kilo similar to 2025. It is not a market price: the average value per kilo depends on the mix of animals exported (calves, young bulls, adults), which can change from one year to the next. The figures also do not say the cause of the decline, nor the destination of the animals that are no longer exported. For reference, cattle slaughter in Portuguese slaughterhouses was also lower in January–August 2026 (228,350 head) than in the same period of 2025 (246,450 head), according to Eurostat (apro_mt_pheadm). These are questions for the Observatory.

The values may be revised by Eurostat; any revision is recorded in our history.
