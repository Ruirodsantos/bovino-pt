## The history

The Cachena lived for centuries isolated in the high mountains, in small herds, and only acquired a name in the official literature in 1981, in a monograph on Portuguese cattle. It is thought to have evolved from the same animals that gave rise to the Barrosã, but selected by cold and scarcity: only the hardiest survived, able to endure harsh winters in the mountains without human help. Zootechnical registration began in 1994, and the herd book, which recognised it as a native breed, in 1998. In 2010 the herd barely exceeded 2,500 females and 140 males.

## How to recognise it

It is small: the height at the withers can be below 1.10 m. The round-sectioned horns grow upwards and outwards like a corkscrew, unlike the lyre of the Barrosã. The head is long with a straight profile. The coat is light brown, and in winter it is covered in a thick fleece.

## How it is raised

It is a high-mountain breed, with a wild temperament and a strong maternal instinct. Its homeland lies in the Peneda-Gerês National Park, between Castro Laboreiro and the Serra da Mourela.

## Today

The catalogue considers it at risk of extinction. The animals are spread over 10 districts and 26 municipalities, predominantly in Viana do Castelo (Arcos de Valdevez is the centre), but also with a sizeable herd in the South (Beja and Évora).
