## The history

The Garvonesa comes from the transition zones between the plains of the Alentejo and the hills of the Algarve. The name comes from the Garvão fair. It was known as «gado chamusco» or «farrusco», because of its dark coat with its characteristic distribution. It was valued for its hardiness and good temperament in fieldwork, and when mechanisation arrived it was gradually replaced by breeds that produced more meat. It came close to extinction.

Since 1994, the Associação de Agricultores do Campo Branco and the Parque Natural do Sudoeste Alentejano e Costa Vicentina have worked on its conservation, and the herd has been growing slowly.

## How to recognise it

In females, a reddish-brown coat, with black muzzle, withers and extremities and a lighter back. In males, black predominates. The horns are white, darkened at the tips.

## How it is raised

In an extensive system, grazing on natural resources. Most breeders also use sown pastures, and hay, cereals and concentrates when pasture runs short.

## Today

The herds are mainly in Santiago do Cacém, Odemira, Ourique, Castro Verde, Almodôvar, Barrancos, Vila Nova de São Bento and Alcáçovas. The catalogue says the number of animals is still insufficient to launch an improvement programme, and that the breed remains among the severely threatened.
