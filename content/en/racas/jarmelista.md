## The history

In 1870, the livestock census described the Jarmelista breed, concentrated in the former municipality of Jarmelo (now part of Guarda), as the best in the district, perhaps in the country. Then, over about fifty years, it declined so sharply that in 1954 one author gave it up as extinct. What saved it was the persistence of the people of the region and of the breeders' association (Acriguarda), who identified the animals with the characteristics of the breed. In 2006, a study of 185 morphological traits in females and 170 in males showed that they form a distinct group, independent of all the other native breeds.

## How to recognise it

It is a long-bodied bovine, of large stature and fine skeleton, with a pale yellow coat and darker extremities. Males are usually darker. The horns are medium-sized, pale and dark-tipped. The legs are tall.

## How it is raised

In an extensive system, in the mountains, with high-altitude pastures, wide temperature swings and many small farms with few animals. It is very hardy and docile, and withstands better than other breeds raised in the same system, according to the catalogue.

## Today

The herd book records 25 males and 193 females among 25 breeders (2019).
