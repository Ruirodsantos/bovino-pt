## The history

The Algarvia is a rare case: in 2004, the national report on animal genetic resources classed it as one of the breeds that became extinct in Portugal in the twentieth century. The reason lay in breeders' practice: crossing the cows with breeds such as the Limousine and the Charolais to obtain fast-growing calves, the so-called «chamuscos». Over the generations, the pure animals gradually disappeared.

In 2005 a recovery project began. It found 43 females and 4 males, with enough variability to represent a population genetically distinct from the other native breeds of the south.

## How to recognise it

A uniform red or brown coat, with short hair. The head is large and pyramidal in shape; the horns are little developed and grow backwards and outwards in a spiral. The limbs are short and strong.

## How it is raised

The traditional homeland runs from Aljezur and the Monchique plateau, through the Barrocal, to the municipality of Castro Marim and the right bank of the Guadiana. It is a hardy breed, raised by continuous grazing on spontaneous vegetation and adapted to poor soils.

## Today

In 2019 the adult herd book had only 2 males and 2 females of pure line, held by 3 breeders. This is the smallest herd of all the registered native cattle breeds, and it justifies the classification of the breed as critically endangered.
