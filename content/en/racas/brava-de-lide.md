## The history

The Brava de Lide is unlike every other native breed: it was selected not for its body but for its character. The catalogue associates its origin with the aurochs (uro), the wild cattle that survived in Europe until the seventeenth century; this is a thesis about origin, not a direct lineage demonstrated in this text. In Portugal, the most aggressive animals that turned up among local cattle eventually gave rise to a line of their own, cultivated for courage, and the bullfight (lide) became the breed's reason for being.

## How to recognise it

It has a fine skeleton, thin skin, a well-muscled trunk, little dewlap and fine, hook-shaped horns. The coat colours are highly varied and are part of the vocabulary of the breeding trade. The average weights given in the catalogue are about 500 kg for males and 280 kg for females.

## How it is raised

It lives on large estates, in plains and valleys with water and pasture. Breeding is done by bloodlines, the «encastes», and selection is based on behavioural trials.

## Today

The breeding regions are the Alentejo, the Ribatejo and Oeste, the Beira Interior and the Beira Litoral, and the Azores. This breed has a cultural and ecological function of its own; the Atlas does not deal with its meat.
