## The history

From the middle of the nineteenth century there are records of black cattle under names such as «Gado Charnequeiro do Sul do Tejo» (heathland cattle of the south of the Tejo) and «Gado da Terra» (local cattle). They were mainly draught animals, valued for their strength and for tolerating poor feed and extreme climate, and for their dung, which fertilised the land; meat counted for little. With agricultural modernisation they lost their function, and in the 1980s the arrival of exotic breeds reduced the herd further, to the point that the breed was considered close to extinction. It survived thanks to breeders who kept nuclei of pure females to produce crossbreds.

## How to recognise it

A coat tending towards uniform black, sometimes brown or brindled. It is an animal of large build: cows of 400 to 600 kg and bulls of 700 to 1,000 kg. The horns are in a high hook. Males have a large crest.

## How it is raised

Today the aim is almost solely meat, in an extensive system. It feeds on natural pastures, generally under the cover of the montado (cork-oak and holm-oak woodland), on stubble, acorns and thinnings from the montado. Breeders cite adaptation to disadvantaged areas as the main advantage.
