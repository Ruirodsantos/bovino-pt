## The history

The Maronesa is a mountain breed, primitive and hardy. Its official name comes from the Serra do Marão, but the catalogue says that the more fitting name would be Alvanesa, because its true homeland is the Serra do Alvão. Within the region it is called «Serrana». Outside it, in areas of expansion, it became known as «Penato», because of Ribeira de Pena, a trading centre, and as «gado carreiro» (cart cattle), for its capacity for work.

In 1919 Bernardo Lima attributed its origin to a cross between the Barrosã and the Mirandesa. Molecular genetics studies of recent years point, according to the catalogue, to a link with the aurochs (uro); the Atlas has not confirmed the original studies, so the link stands as a hypothesis attributed to the catalogue and not as established fact. When mechanisation put an end to the demand for working animals, the bet to save the breed was meat.

## How to recognise it

A short, lean head with a broad forehead. The horns come out horizontally and then turn forwards and downwards, with the tips curving up. The original colour is black with a reddish stripe along the back and loins; today brown females predominate.

## How it is raised

In a complex system tied to the terrain and to small landholdings: semi-housing and grazing for adult animals, permanent housing with manger feeding for the young.
