## The history

The name comes from Miranda do Douro and the Terras de Miranda, the centre from which the breed spread. Its remote origin is debated: some derive it from wild cattle, others link it to a central European stock with a concave head, and others speak of a mixture. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries three types were distinguished: the Mirandesa proper (municipalities of Miranda do Douro, Mogadouro and Vimioso), the Bragancesa, darker and a better milker (Bragança and Vinhais), and the Beiroa, paler (Beiras). Today even the Bragancesa's darker coat is no longer seen. From the 1970s onwards, mechanisation and exotic breeds drove the Mirandesa back, until by the early 1990s it was found almost only in its homeland. Afterwards, the herd declined in the homeland and grew outside it, mainly in the Alentejo and the Beiras.

## How to recognise it

It is a large animal: cows weigh about 500 kg and bulls 900 kg. The coat is fair on the back and on the marrafa (forelock) and darkens to black at the extremities. Males are darker; calves are uniformly fair. The horns are whitish, darkened at the tip.

## How it is raised

Two systems coexist: traditional semi-housing and the extensive system, which is the only one outside the homeland and is growing within it. In the extensive system, calves stay with their mothers at pasture until 5 to 7 months; they are then weaned and fattened on hay and cereals from the farm itself. The catalogue mentions the Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) «Carne Mirandesa», which will be confirmed in eAmbrosia.
