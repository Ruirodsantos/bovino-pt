## The history

For a long time it was called Minhota or Galega. At the start of zootechnical registration the name Galega was adopted; it lasted until 2002 and is still common among breeders. To distinguish it from the Spanish Rubia Gallega, the official name became Minhota. The herd book began in 1997, and from 2001 semen from Minhota bulls began to be produced; in 2002 the danger of extinction was recognised and breeders were able to apply for agri-environmental measures.

## How to recognise it

The coat is fawn (flavo), from light red to straw. The horns, of medium size, come out almost horizontally and bend backwards and then forwards. It is a breed of medium to large build, with a rectangular silhouette.

## The meat

Here the catalogue is more concrete than for other breeds. The main product is veal, sold on average at 6.2 ± 1.15 months; 52.5% of slaughtered Minhota animals yield category V carcasses (animals under 8 months), with an average carcass weight of 149.6 ± 31.41 kg. The production of castrated males, long traditional in some regions, tends to be recovering: the animals are castrated between 6 and 8 months, reach great size and are normally slaughtered after three years of age. The carcass has excellent conformation and the meat is highly tender. The catalogue points to exports to Spain, which absorbs a large part of production, as one of the reasons for the recovery of castrated males. These values are from the catalogue (2019 or earlier).
