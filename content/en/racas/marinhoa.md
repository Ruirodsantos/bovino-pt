## The history

The Marinhoa is tied to the «marinhas» of the Beira Litoral, the low, fertile lands of the estuary of the Vouga, the Cértima and the Antuã. It was a powerful, long-legged animal, ideal for rice growing, for gathering moliço (seaweed) in the lagoon and for the Arte Xávega (beach seine fishing). It was present in almost every household, and helped to keep people in the villages of a region of small plots. Mechanisation reduced the animals' work, and the dairy basin that developed in the region replaced many Marinhoas with dairy cows. It survived because many families kept a few animals as an economic supplement.

## How to recognise it

It is a large animal: about 135 cm tall at the withers, and 600 to 700 kg in adult females, 900 to 1,000 kg in males. The coat is yellow, in straw shades, sometimes more cherry-red, with darker hairs on the edge of the ears, on the dewlap, around the eyes and at the tip of the tail. It is docile and easy to handle.

## How it is raised

The most traditional system is intensive outdoor rearing: grazing for some months of the year and a daily return to the byre. Feeding is based on natural or sown pastures, supplemented with cereals, straw and legumes.

## Today

The meat aptitude is the one mainly exploited; work is residual.
