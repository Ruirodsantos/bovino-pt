## The history

The Arouquesa is a mountain breed. It has never strayed far from the territory where it originated: the slopes between the Vouga valley and the Douro. The name may come from the aurochs, the wild ancestor of cattle, now extinct, or from the town of Arouca; authors differ. In the past, local varieties were recognised, with names such as Sulanos (S. Pedro do Sul), Paivotos (river Paiva), Caramuleiros (Serra do Caramulo) and Canaveses (Marco de Canaveses and Baião).

## How to recognise it

It is a small to medium-sized breed, with rounded forms and a docile but lively expression. The coat is brown, from straw to cherry-red, darker around the eyes and ears. The horns are shaped like a low lyre, pale for most of their length with dark tips.

## How it is raised

It lives on the poor mountain pastures and on shoots of gorse, carqueja, broom and oak. Systems vary: grazing on common land (rare today), permanent housing, housing only in winter, or a semi-extensive regime in which the animals only sleep in the byre. The catalogue highlights three aptitudes: meat, work and milk.

## Today

The municipalities with the most animals are Cinfães, Castro Daire, S. Pedro do Sul, Arouca, Vale de Cambra, Resende and Castelo de Paiva, south of the Douro, and Baião, Amarante and Marco de Canaveses, to the north.
