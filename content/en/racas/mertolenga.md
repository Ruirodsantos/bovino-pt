## The history

The Mertolenga comes from the Baixo Alentejo, from the lands of Mértola, Alcoutim and Martim Longo. It was known as the «boi de cabresto» (halter ox): the tame animal that accompanied the fighting cattle in the Ribatejo. Around 1900, when the border was opened to livestock from the two Iberian countries, spotted cattle came from Spain, with a reputation for hardiness. In the first decades of the twentieth century, the rice-growing areas of the Tejo, the Sado and the Sorraia needed an energetic animal that the other breeds did not supply, and the red bragado type emerged, linked to Coruche. The blending of several lines, with a roan type («mil-flores»), consolidated the breed, and the Herdade da Abóboda served as the site of standardisation.

## How to recognise it

The coat may be red, roan, spotted red or red-spotted. The horns are fine, white, dark-tipped, hooked or in a low lyre. It is a medium-sized animal with a fine skeleton and a nervous temperament.

## How it is raised

The catalogue highlights its hardiness, ease of calving, productive longevity and good maternal ability, which makes it a good mother for maternal lines.
