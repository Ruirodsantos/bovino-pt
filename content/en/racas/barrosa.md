## The history

The origin of the Barrosã is much debated. One of the hypotheses the catalogue mentions links it to North African cattle with large, thick horns, which would have reached the Iberian Peninsula during the Moorish presence. It would later have been pushed by other groups of cattle into the highlands of Barroso, where it stayed. Whatever its origin, it is a breed of very distinctive morphology, hard to fit among the other Iberian breeds.

## How to recognise it

The thick, long horns, in a high lyre, are what one sees first. The coat is light brown, between straw and cherry-red, and the skin is thick but soft, with folds on the neck. The dewlap is very well developed. It is a docile breed with a strong maternal instinct.

## How it is raised

It is raised in an extensive system, in the mountains and on mid-slopes, making use of resources such as carqueja and gorse. The catalogue stresses its role in the rural landscape: it worked small plots and terraces, and supplied manure for poor soils.

## Today

The catalogue states that the Barrosã is increasingly raised for meat, and mentions the Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) «Carne Barrosã». The exact registration of the designation will be confirmed in the European eAmbrosia database before it is shown as verified data.
