## The history

The settlement of the Azores in the fifteenth century brought cattle from various regions of the mainland. On Terceira, the first settlers were of Minho and Algarve origin, and brought the animals they knew. The catalogue also cites that the breeds introduced into the Azores were the Alentejana, the Mirandesa, the Minhota and the Algarvia, which, through insularity and isolation, gave rise to a reddish type, the «raça da terra» (local breed), from which the Ramo Grande emerged. The herd book was created in 1996 and is managed by the Direção Regional da Agricultura dos Açores.

## How to recognise it

Animals of enormous stature, with a strong skeleton and broad joints, with the forequarters more developed, which is linked to a breed of exceptional working qualities. The coat is red, more or less intense. On Terceira, fawn red predominates; on São Jorge, cherry red.

## How it is raised

At pasture all year round. The breeder seeks to match the spring grass peaks to the animals' needs, and calvings are therefore concentrated from February to May. Today it is raised mainly for meat.
