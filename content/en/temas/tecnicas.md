- title: Wood or charcoal embers.
  text: 'The most traditional in Portugal, with the *posta mirandesa* as its symbol.


    Strong fire, thick piece, turn it few times, coarse salt at the end.'
  photo: tecnicas-cena
- title: Cast-iron frying pan.
  text: High heat, little fat, dry piece. Add butter, garlic and thyme only at the end, with the heat lowered.
- title: Reverse sear (*reverse sear*).
  text: 'Thick pieces go first into a low oven (about 100 to 120 °C) until they are some 8 to 10 °C below the target doneness, and only then are seared over a strong flame.


    It gives an even doneness from edge to edge.'
- title: Stew.
  text: Meat that is seared, then cooked slowly and covered in liquid (wine, stock) over a very gentle heat, for hours.
- title: Low temperature and sous-vide.
  text: 'A restaurant technique in which the piece cooks for many hours at a low, controlled temperature.


    Safety does not depend on temperature alone: what counts is the temperature actually reached at the centre, the exposure time, the thickness, the preparation (whole piece or minced), storage before and after, and subsequent treatment. Do not use generic temperatures as a guarantee: follow time and temperature combinations validated by official food safety sources (for example, USDA/FSIS tables, to be checked) and, in catering, the establishment''s HACCP plan.'
- title: And how do you know the doneness?
  text: In a demanding restaurant the doneness is not guessed. Use a **probe thermometer** and measure at the centre, without touching the bone.
