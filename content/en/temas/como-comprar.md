- title: What does it smell like?
  text: 'Fresh beef smells of very little, and clean: slightly metallic and milky.


    Dry-aged meat has aromas of walnut, aged cheese and mushroom and, with long ageing, of ripe fruit and broth.


    Sweet-sour, sour or ammonia smells are a sign of spoilage.'
- title: What does the label say?
  text: 'In the European Union, beef must be labelled with traceability information under Regulation (EC) No 1760/2000:


    - reference number;

    - country of birth, fattening and slaughter;

    - approval number of the abattoir and the cutting plant.'
- title: And if the butcher doesn't know?
  text: If the butcher cannot tell you where the cut comes from, **there is a serious failing**.
- title: Veal, young bullock, ox or cow?
  text: 'The age and category of the animal make an enormous difference to flavour.


    The European carcass classification system (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/1182) grades conformation (S, E, U, R, O, P) and fat cover (1 to 5). It is a commercial tool and not an index of culinary quality, but it helps you understand what you are buying.


    [See how classification works →](../classificacao/)'
- title: The checklist.
  text: '- Colour of the interior: bright red after a few minutes in the air.

    - Surface moist but not slimy.

    - Firm fat, with no rancid smell.

    - A clean smell.

    - Complete traceability on the label.

    - Cut kept at a temperature between 0 and 4 °C from the source.'
