- title: Why does the meat turn brown?
  text: 'At the surface, when the temperature rises above about **140 °C** and the meat is dry, the **Maillard reaction** takes place: sugars and amino acids react and create hundreds of aroma compounds, the brown colour and the "grilled" flavour.'
  photo: crosta-cena
- title: Why dry the meat first?
  text: Because water is the enemy of the crust. As long as there is water on the surface, the temperature cannot go past 100 °C, and the Maillard reaction does not begin.
- title: And if the pan is cold?
  text: 'A pan that is not hot enough, or crowded with steaks, produces **grey meat**: the water that comes out stops the surface from getting past 100 °C.'
