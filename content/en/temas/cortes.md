- title: The golden rule.
  text: '**The more a muscle works, the more flavour it has and the longer it takes to cook.**


    The supporting and moving muscles (neck, shoulder, leg) are rich in collagen and coarse fibre. Those that hardly work, next to the spine, are tender and milder in flavour.'
  photo: cortes-cena
- title: Beware of names.
  text: 'Cut names vary between regions of Portugal and between butchers. The table below uses the most common names and gives the anatomical equivalent; **always check with your butcher**.


    Spanish, French, British or Brazilian cuts should not be translated literally: the same name can mean different cuts. The location of *vazia*, *lombo*, *lombinho* and *posta* is still to be validated with a professional butcher.'
  diagram: cuts
- title: The cuts, one by one.
  text: '| Cut | Where it sits | Character | Ideal method |

    |---|---|---|---|

    | **Lombinho** (tenderloin) | Inside the loin, next to the spine | The most tender, with the mildest flavour | Grill or frying pan, quick, rare to medium-rare |

    | **Lombo alto** (with or without bone) | Back, front part | Marbled and flavourful; it is the boneless "rib steak" or the rib chop | Charcoal, frying pan, oven after searing |

    | **Lombo baixo** (short loin) | Back, rear part | Firm fibre, covering fat | Grill; always slice against the grain |

    | **Vazia** (flank-type steak) | Region of the loin next to the ribs (to be validated with a butcher) | Coarse fibre and good flavour | Quick grill, slice thinly against the grain |

    | **Aba** (flank) | Flank (abdominal wall) | Long fibre and plenty of flavour | Quick grill or braise; slice [against the grain](../contra-a-fibra/) |

    | **Alcatra** (rump) | Rump | Tender, lean, versatile | Grill, roasts, *posta* |

    | **Posta** (thick steak) | Thick cut, traditionally from the rump or loin | Traditional cut; the *posta mirandesa* is the benchmark | Wood or charcoal embers |

    | **Acém e cachaço** (chuck and neck) | Neck and shoulder | Plenty of flavour, plenty of collagen | Stews and slow-cooked boiled dishes |

    | **Pá** (shoulder) | Shoulder | Coarse fibre and intense flavour | Braise, shred |

    | **Peito** (brisket) | Lower part of the chest | Fatty and collagen-rich | Long cooking, smoking |

    | **Chambão** (shin) | Leg (shank) | The bone with marrow and muscle full of collagen; cut across in slices it gives *ossobuco* | Braise |

    | **Rabo** (oxtail) | Tail | Gelatinous | Braise for hours |'
- title: Is bone-in better?
  text: 'The bone conducts heat differently, protects the meat around it and, during ageing, helps the cut hold up.


    The bone-in chop (lombo alto) is a prime cut for ageing.'
- title: How thick?
  text: 'For charcoal and frying pan, a steak for two should be **at least 3 to 4 cm** thick. Thinner, and it dries out before it forms a crust.
