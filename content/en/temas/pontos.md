- title: Cooking is not «burning until done».
  text: It is steering a set of physical and chemical changes that happen at different temperatures. Those who know them **choose the doneness**, instead of leaving it to chance.
- title: What happens at each temperature?
  text: '- **Around 40 to 50 °C**: the meat warms up, but is still raw inside.

    - **Close to 50 to 55 °C**: myosin, one of the main proteins, begins to coagulate. The meat loses its translucency and starts to firm up.

    - **60 °C and above**: collagen (the connective tissue) begins to shrink and expel liquid; the meat becomes firmer and loses juiciness.

    - **Close to 65 to 75 °C**: actin, another protein, coagulates. The meat dries out and turns «like shoe leather» if there is no fat or gelatine.

    - **Above about 70 °C, for a long time**: collagen slowly converts into **gelatine**, and working-muscle meat becomes tender and unctuous. This is the principle of the stew.'
  photo: pontos-cena
- title: Two practical conclusions.
  text: '1. **Tender steaks** (tenderloin, loin): cook briefly, so as not to pass the point where the meat dries out.

    2. **Working cuts** (chuck, shoulder, shin, oxtail): cook for a long time, slowly, in liquid, to give the gelatine time.'
- title: What is the right doneness?
  text: 'Temperature at the centre of the piece:


    | Doneness | Temperature at the centre | Appearance |

    |---|---|---|

    | Rare (*bleu/rare*) | about 48 to 52 °C | Bright red, almost cold in the centre |

    | Medium-rare (*medium-rare*) | about 54 to 57 °C | Pinkish red, warm and moist; the doneness most chefs choose for quality meat |

    | Medium (*medium*) | about 58 to 62 °C | Pink, still juicy |

    | Well done | above 68 to 70 °C | Grey, firm, drier |'
- title: And safety?
  text: 'These values are those commonly used in professional kitchens. Food safety authorities, such as the USDA in the United States, recommend an internal temperature of 63 °C followed by 3 minutes of rest for whole cuts of beef, and 71 °C for minced meat.


    The reason: in minced meat, bacteria from the surface end up mixed into the interior. In a whole steak, little or nothing passes into the muscle; it is the surface that is sterilised by the crust.


    Anyone serving rare meat to customers takes on that responsibility and should buy meat of controlled origin.'
