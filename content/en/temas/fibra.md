scenes:
- title: What can you see in the cut?
  text: Look at the cross-section. A **fine, dense** grain points to a muscle that does little work, such as the tenderloin (*lombinho*), and to **tender** meat.
- title: And if the grain is coarse?
  text: 'A **coarse grain, with plenty of visible connective tissue**, points to a working muscle, such as the shoulder (*pá*), the brisket (*peito*) or the shin (*chambão*).


    These cuts call for **long cooking**.'
- title: And to the touch?
  text: Press the piece with the pad of your finger. It should feel firm, springy and slightly moist, with no milky liquid and no slimy film.
- title: How do you slice it after cooking?
  text: 'In any steak, the muscle fibres run in one direction. **Slicing against the grain** (across it) shortens the fibres you chew and gives a feeling of tenderness.


    It matters most for flank (*vazia*), the lower loin and coarse-grained cuts.
