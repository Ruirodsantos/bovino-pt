The plate is the last stage and the one the customer sees. A good product, aged and cooked with rigour, is lost on a cold plate or on a cut sliced in the wrong direction.

## Before serving

- **Warm plates**, not cold. Meat served on a cold plate cools within minutes.
- **Resting completed** (see [cooking](../04-cozedura/)).
- **Flaky salt** (*sal em flor*) or coarse salt at the last moment, for crunch and contrast.
- **The cut's own fat**: with aged cuts, the melted cover fat can accompany the dish.

## Carving

Large cuts, such as certain *costeletas* (bone-in rib steaks) or *postas* (thick-cut steaks), can be presented whole and carved in front of the customer, depending on the service concept. When this is done: strips of 1 to 1.5 cm, against the grain, with the bone on the plate as presentation.

## Accompaniments

Quality meat calls for simple accompaniments that do not hide it:

- Potatoes (roasted in a wood oven, boiled in their skins, or fried in olive oil).
- Grilled vegetables or a salad of bitter leaves.
- Short sauces: a reduction of the meat's own juices, herb butter, mustard.
- For braised cuts: purée, *migas*, oven-baked rice (*arroz de forno*), turnip tops (*grelos*).

## Wines

Principle: lean, tender meat calls for finer wines; fatty, intense meat calls for more body and tannins.

- **Lombinho (the small loin), alcatra (rump), lean steaks**: medium-bodied reds, such as elegant Douro or Dão, or Bairrada from Baga with good acidity.
- **Lombo alto (high loin), costeleta, aged posta**: structured reds from the Douro or the Alentejo, preferably with some years of age.
- **Stews and working-muscle meat**: reds with body and good acidity; the wine from the stew itself is usually the best accompaniment.

These are suggestions of principle, not fixed recipes.

## The standard the Atlas sets itself

The restaurant El Capricho, in Jiménez de Jamuz (León), serves as a reference of ambition: an animal raised with a purpose, long ageing, a single well-worked cut. The Atlas do Bovino (Portuguese Beef Atlas) aims to gather, in Portuguese, the knowledge needed to reach that level with Portuguese beef: native breeds, breeders, ageing, cuts and cooking.

This demands work that cannot be done in a text: tastings, measurements, visits to breeders and ageing chambers, conversations with chefs and butchers. They are planned in the project's research list and will be published only when verified.
