The golden rule: **the harder a muscle works, the more flavour it has and the more time it needs**. The muscles of support and movement (neck, shoulder, leg) are rich in collagen and coarse fibre; those that hardly work (next to the spine) are tender and more discreet in flavour.

Cut names vary between regions of Portugal and between butchers. This table uses the most common names and gives the anatomical equivalent; always check with your butcher. Spanish, French, British or Brazilian cuts must not be translated literally: the same name can designate different cuts. The location of *vazia*, *lombo*, *lombinho* and *posta* was corrected after an audit and has still to be validated with a professional butcher.

## The cuts

| Cut | Where it sits | Character | Ideal method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lombinho** | Inside the *lombo* (loin), next to the spine | The most tender, with the mildest flavour | Grill or frying pan, quickly, rare to medium-rare |
| **Lombo alto** (with or without bone) | Back, front part | Marbled and flavourful; it is the boneless "rib steak" or the *costeleta* (bone-in) | Embers, frying pan, oven after searing |
| **Lombo baixo** | Back, rear part | Firm fibre, cover fat | Grill; always slice against the grain |
| **Vazia** | Region of the loin next to the ribs (the exact anatomical equivalence varies; **to be validated with a butcher**) | Coarse fibre and good flavour | Quick grill, slice thinly against the grain |
| **Aba** | Flank (abdominal wall) | Long fibre and plenty of flavour | Quick grill or braise; slice against the grain |
| **Alcatra** | Rump | Tender, lean, versatile | Grill, roasts, *posta* |
| **Posta** | Thick cut, traditionally from the *alcatra* or *lombo* | Traditional cut; the *posta mirandesa* is the reference | Wood or charcoal embers |
| **Acém e cachaço** | Neck and shoulder | Plenty of flavour, plenty of collagen | Stews and slow-cooked dishes |
| **Pá** | Shoulder | Coarse fibre and intense flavour | Braise, shred |
| **Peito** | Lower part of the breast | Fatty and collagen-rich | Long cooking, smoking |
| **Chambão** | Leg (shin) | The bone with marrow and a muscle full of collagen; cut in cross-section it gives the *ossobuco* | Braise |
| **Rabo** | Tail | Gelatinous | Braise for hours |

## Reading the grain

In any steak, the muscle fibres run in one direction. **Slicing against the grain** (across) shortens the fibres you chew and gives a sensation of tenderness. It matters above all for *vazia*, *lombo baixo* and coarse-fibred meats.

## Bone: dearer and better?

Bone conducts heat differently, protects the meat around it and, during ageing, helps keep the cut in good condition. Halfway between tradition and technique, the bone-in *costeleta* (*lombo alto*) is a prime cut for ageing.

## Thickness

For embers and frying pan, a steak for two should be at least 3 to 4 cm. Any thinner and it dries out before it develops a crust.
